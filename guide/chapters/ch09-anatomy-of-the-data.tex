\chapter{Anatomy of the Data}
\label{ch:anatomy-of-the-data}

\textit{A single decision in \sysname{} touches three databases and a cache.
This chapter follows a concrete example---``Use PostgreSQL as the primary
database''---from raw conversation through extraction, storage, embedding, and
caching.  Understanding where data lives is the key to debugging anything in
the system.}


% ─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
\section{The Raw Conversation}
\label{sec:raw-conversation}

Everything starts with a conversation between a developer and the AI interview
agent.  Below is a condensed version of what the capture session from
Chapter~\ref{ch:your-first-session} might produce:

\begin{lstlisting}[style=bash, numbers=none, caption={A capture session about database selection (abridged).}]
Agent:  What decision would you like to capture today?

User:   We need to select a primary database for our new
        transaction processing service.

Agent:  What prompted this decision?

User:   The team is starting a new fintech microservice that
        handles payment processing.  We need strong consistency
        guarantees.

Agent:  What alternatives did you consider?

User:   PostgreSQL, MongoDB, and CockroachDB.

Agent:  What did you decide, and why?

User:   We chose PostgreSQL.  It has the best ecosystem support,
        our team already knows it, and ACID compliance is critical
        for financial transactions.  MongoDB's eventual consistency
        model was a dealbreaker.
\end{lstlisting}

This conversation is processed in real time by the extraction pipeline.  By the
time the user confirms the summary in stage seven, the system has already
identified the decision structure and the entities involved.

\section{The Extracted Trace}
\label{sec:extracted-trace}

The extraction service (\codefile{apps/api/services/extractor.py}) sends the
conversation to the LLM with a structured prompt that asks for five fields.
The result is a \emph{decision trace}:

\begin{table}[ht]
\centering
\caption{The extracted decision trace for our example.}
\label{tab:extracted-trace}
\begin{tabularx}{\textwidth}{l X}
\toprule
\textbf{Field} & \textbf{Value} \\
\midrule
Trigger    & Need to select a primary database for a new transaction processing service. \\
Context    & Fintech microservice handling payment processing; team requires strong
             consistency and ACID compliance. \\
Options    & PostgreSQL, MongoDB, CockroachDB. \\
Decision   & Use PostgreSQL as the primary database. \\
Rationale  & Best ecosystem support, existing team expertise, ACID compliance critical
             for financial transactions; MongoDB's eventual consistency was a
             dealbreaker. \\
Confidence & 0.92 \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabularx}
\end{table}

The confidence score (0.92) indicates that the LLM found clear, well-articulated
reasoning in the conversation.  Scores below 0.7 typically mean the rationale
was vague or the decision was not explicitly stated.  The extraction also
identifies three entity mentions: \emph{PostgreSQL}, \emph{MongoDB}, and
\emph{CockroachDB}.

\section{PostgreSQL Storage}
\label{sec:postgres-storage}

The decision trace is persisted as a row in the \ic{decision\_traces} table.
PostgreSQL stores the structured metadata and serves as the system of record for
relational queries (filtering by user, project, date range, status).

\begin{table}[ht]
\centering
\caption{The relational row in PostgreSQL (selected columns).}
\label{tab:postgres-row}
\begin{tabularx}{\textwidth}{l X}
\toprule
\textbf{Column} & \textbf{Value} \\
\midrule
\ic{id}           & \ic{a3f7c2e1-...} (UUID, primary key) \\
\ic{user\_id}     & \ic{b8d4a1f0-...} (foreign key to \ic{users}) \\
\ic{project\_id}  & \ic{c5e9b3d2-...} (foreign key to \ic{projects}) \\
\ic{source}       & \ic{"interview"} \\
\ic{status}       & \ic{"confirmed"} \\
\ic{trigger}      & \ic{"Need to select a primary database..."} \\
\ic{decision}     & \ic{"Use PostgreSQL as the primary database"} \\
\ic{confidence}   & \ic{0.92} \\
\ic{created\_at}  & \ic{2026-04-04T14:23:17Z} \\
\ic{updated\_at}  & \ic{2026-04-04T14:23:17Z} \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabularx}
\end{table}

The \ic{source} column tracks how the decision entered the system:
\ic{"interview"} for the AI-guided capture, \ic{"import"} for bulk JSON uploads,
or \ic{"auto"} for automatic extraction from conversation logs.
The full text fields (context, options, rationale) are stored in additional
columns not shown above.

\section{Neo4j Storage}
\label{sec:neo4j-storage}

Simultaneously, the decision and its entities are written to the Neo4j knowledge
graph.  You can query the decision directly with Cypher:

\begin{lstlisting}[style=cypher, numbers=none, caption={Finding the decision in Neo4j.}]
MATCH (d:DecisionTrace)
WHERE d.trigger STARTS WITH 'Need to select a primary database'
RETURN d
\end{lstlisting}

The \ic{DecisionTrace} node carries all five trace fields as properties, plus
metadata like \ic{user\_id}, \ic{confidence}, and \ic{created\_at}.  The node
also stores the 2048-dimensional embedding vector (discussed in
Section~\ref{sec:embedding-storage}).

The three entities are stored as \ic{Entity} nodes, connected to the decision
via \ic{INVOLVES} edges:

\begin{lstlisting}[style=cypher, numbers=none, caption={Viewing the decision and its entity relationships.}]
MATCH (d:DecisionTrace)-[r:INVOLVES]->(e:Entity)
WHERE d.trigger STARTS WITH 'Need to select a primary database'
RETURN d.decision, type(r), e.name, e.type
\end{lstlisting}

This returns three rows:

\begin{table}[ht]
\centering
\caption{The \ic{INVOLVES} edges for our example decision.}
\label{tab:involves-edges}
\begin{tabular}{l l l}
\toprule
\textbf{Decision} & \textbf{Relationship} & \textbf{Entity} \\
\midrule
Use PostgreSQL\ldots & INVOLVES & PostgreSQL (Database) \\
Use PostgreSQL\ldots & INVOLVES & MongoDB (Database) \\
Use PostgreSQL\ldots & INVOLVES & CockroachDB (Database) \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{table}

If another decision also involves PostgreSQL, both decisions connect to the
\emph{same} \ic{Entity} node---this is the entity resolution at work (covered in
detail in Chapter~\ref{ch:entity-resolution-pipeline}).  The shared node becomes
a hub that lets you traverse from one decision to related decisions through their
common entities.

\section{Embedding Storage}
\label{sec:embedding-storage}

Every decision trace is embedded into a 2048-dimensional vector using NVIDIA's
NV-EmbedQA model (\ic{nvidia/llama-3.2-nv-embedqa-1b-v2}).  The embedding is a
weighted combination of the trace fields:

\begin{table}[ht]
\centering
\caption{Embedding field weights (configured in \codefile{config.py}).}
\label{tab:embedding-weights}
\begin{tabular}{l c}
\toprule
\textbf{Field} & \textbf{Weight} \\
\midrule
Title     & 1.5$\times$ \\
Decision  & 1.2$\times$ \\
Rationale & 1.0$\times$ (base) \\
Context   & 0.8$\times$ \\
Trigger   & 0.8$\times$ \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{table}

The resulting vector is stored as a property on the \ic{DecisionTrace} node in
Neo4j and indexed for approximate nearest-neighbor search.  When you use the
hybrid search or Ask features, the system embeds your query with the same model
and finds the closest decision vectors by cosine similarity.

Embeddings are also cached in Redis to avoid redundant API calls.  The cache key
follows the pattern \ic{emb:nvembed:passage:\{md5\_hash\}} with a 30-day TTL.


% ─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
\section{Redis Cache}
\label{sec:redis-cache}

Redis serves as a multi-purpose cache layer.  For our example decision, three
types of cache entries are relevant:

\begin{table}[ht]
\centering
\caption{Redis cache keys related to a single decision.}
\label{tab:redis-keys}
\begin{tabularx}{\textwidth}{l c X}
\toprule
\textbf{Key Pattern} & \textbf{TTL} & \textbf{Purpose} \\
\midrule
\ic{entity:\{user\_id\}:exact:postgresql} & 5 min & Entity resolution cache.
    Stores the resolved entity node for ``postgresql'' so subsequent mentions
    skip the 7-stage resolution pipeline. \\
\ic{llm:v1:extract:\{md5\_hash\}} & 24 h & LLM response cache.
    Stores the extraction result so re-processing the same conversation text
    returns instantly.  Invalidated by bumping
    \confkey{LLM\_EXTRACTION\_PROMPT\_VERSION}. \\
\ic{emb:nvembed:passage:\{md5\_hash\}} & 30 d & Embedding cache.
    Stores the 2048-dimensional vector to avoid redundant NVIDIA API calls. \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabularx}
\end{table}

The entity cache is per-user to respect data isolation---user A's resolution of
``postgres'' should not leak into user B's namespace.  The LLM and embedding
caches are global because the same input always produces the same output
regardless of user.

\begin{keyinsight}
A single decision touches three databases and a cache.  PostgreSQL is the system
of record for structured metadata.  Neo4j is the system of record for
relationships and semantic search.  Redis accelerates repeated lookups.
Understanding where data lives---and which system is authoritative---is essential
for debugging any issue in \sysname{}.
\end{keyinsight}
